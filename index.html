01 / ABOUT

JW

Dr. Jiayu Wang
Postdoctoral Researcher

Ecology across
the land–sea boundary.

I am an experimental ecologist at Xiamen University. I study how mangroves and salt-marsh plants interact, persist, and respond to a changing environment.

My research focuses on coastal wetland communities in tropical and subtropical China, especially the mangrove–Spartina alterniflora ecotone. Through controlled field experiments, I examine how environmental gradients and plant interactions shape community patterns, with implications for wetland conservation and invasion management.

Research interests

  • Coastal wetland ecology
  • Plant invasion and coexistence
  • Clonal growth and plasticity
  • Mangrove–salt marsh ecotones

01

PLANT INTERACTIONS

Aboveground & belowground competition

Separating neighboring roots and shoots to understand how competition shapes the mangrove–salt marsh ecotone.

Experimental approach +

The “target technique” separates aboveground and belowground compartments into four treatments: neighboring roots and shoots, shoots only, roots only, and no neighbors.

02

ENVIRONMENTAL GRADIENTS

Salt-marsh mesocosm array

In-situ mesocosms across estuarine sites and tidal elevations reveal how coastal plants respond to salinity and inundation.

Experimental approach +

Mesocosm arrays at multiple sites within an estuary combine tidal elevation gradients with salinity gradients. This controlled field setting links environmental conditions to plant growth and life-history strategies.

THE STUDY SYSTEM

A closer look
at coastal plants.

Mangroves and salt-marsh species
featured in my research.

Spartina alternifloraSmooth cordgrass+

Invasive in China. My research focuses on invasion ecology, phenotypic plasticity, and management in Chinese coastal wetlands.

Kandelia obovataRiver mangrove+

A native mangrove. I investigate its regeneration ecology and conservation.

Phragmites australisCommon reed+

A widespread wetland grass with native and invasive lineages. I study ecological interactions and responses to environmental change.

Scirpus mariqueterSea clubrush+

A native intertidal sedge. I examine competitive interactions with invasive Spartina alterniflora.

Avicennia marinaGrey mangrove+

A salt-tolerant mangrove. I investigate factors affecting regeneration and restoration in southern China.
