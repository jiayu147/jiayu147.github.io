Research

Coastal Wetland Ecology & Conservation Biology

Research Overview

My research focuses on the ecology and conservation of coastal wetland ecosystems, with particular emphasis on plant invasions, clonal growth strategies, and ecosystem responses to environmental change. I combine field observations, controlled experiments, and quantitative analyses to address fundamental and applied questions in coastal ecology.

Coastal wetlands are among the most productive and economically important ecosystems on Earth. However, they face multiple threats from climate change, sea-level rise, and biological invasions. My work aims to inform conservation and management efforts by providing insights into the mechanisms that govern ecosystem structure and function.

Research Interests

🌊 Coastal Wetland Ecology

Understanding the structure, function, and dynamics of coastal wetland ecosystems, including salt marshes, mangroves, and seagrass meadows. I investigate how these ecosystems respond to environmental gradients and anthropogenic disturbances.

🌱 Plant Invasion Ecology

Examining the mechanisms underlying plant invasion success in coastal environments, with a focus on Spartina alterniflora. I study the ecological impacts of invasive species and develop strategies for effective management and restoration.

🌿 Mangrove-Saltmarsh Ecotone Dynamics

Investigating the interactions between mangroves and salt marshes at their ecotone. I study how these two important coastal vegetation types compete, coexist, and respond to environmental change at their boundaries.

🌾 Plant Clonal Growth

Understanding the ecological and evolutionary implications of clonal growth in coastal plants. I investigate how clonal integration, resource sharing, and physiological integration affect plant performance and ecosystem functioning.

💧 Coastal Clonal Salt Marshes

Studying the ecology and ecosystem functioning of coastal clonal salt marshes. I examine how clonal growth strategies affect community assembly, ecosystem productivity, and resilience to environmental change.
