Species

Key Species in My Research

My research focuses on several key plant species that play important roles in coastal wetland ecosystems. Here are some of the most important species I study.

Spartina alterniflora

Common Name: Smooth Cordgrass

Status: Invasive species in China

Ecology: A perennial grass native to the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of North America. It has invaded coastal wetlands in China, where it forms dense monocultures and alters ecosystem processes.

Research Focus: I study the invasion ecology, phenotypic plasticity, and management strategies for this species in Chinese coastal wetlands.

Kandelia obovata

Common Name: River Mangrove

Status: Native species

Ecology: A native mangrove species found in the Indo-West Pacific region. It plays a crucial role in coastal protection and carbon sequestration.

Research Focus: I investigate the regeneration ecology and conservation of this important mangrove species.

Phragmites australis

Common Name: Common Reed

Status: Native and invasive lineages

Ecology: A widespread wetland grass found on every continent except Antarctica. It can form extensive stands in freshwater and brackish wetlands.

Research Focus: I study the ecological interactions between native and invasive lineages and their responses to environmental change.

Scirpus mariqueter

Common Name: Sea Clubrush

Status: Native species

Ecology: A native sedge species found in the intertidal zones of China's coastal wetlands. It is an important component of native plant communities.

Research Focus: I examine the competitive interactions between this native species and invasive Spartina alterniflora.

Avicennia marina

Common Name: Grey Mangrove

Status: Native species

Ecology: A widespread mangrove species found in tropical and subtropical regions. It is highly tolerant of salinity and plays a key role in coastal protection.

Research Focus: I investigate the factors affecting mangrove regeneration and restoration success in southern China.
